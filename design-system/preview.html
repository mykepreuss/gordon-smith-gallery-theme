                About the work

                Marlene Yuen’s work explores place through history, memory, and community. Drawing from archival research and storytelling, she examines how places become sites of cultural exchange and collective belonging. Her serigraph, Short Gumboots (2026) originates from her installation, Peace Together (2024), which pays homage to the historic Hong Wo General Store in Steveston Village, founded in 1895 by Ling Lam.

                Yuen’s image of a simple pair of gumboots represents the many items that were purchased from Hong Wo, reflecting a time where diverse communities lived and worked alongside one another in Steveston’s fishing industry. The boots are a quiet symbol of labour, movement, and connection, reflecting the generations of Indigenous, Chinese, Japanese, and Western settlers who contributed to the identity of the place. Through Yuen’s hands, place emerges as a shared history, built through everyday experiences and sustained through acts of remembrance, harmony, and peace.

                The exhibition frames the Gordon Smith Gallery as a shared space to engage with artwork, educational programming, and interactive installations, providing opportunities to explore the following questions:

                • What role does collecting play in the artistic process?
                • How do the ways we collect and gather from the lands and waters reflect our relationship with the Earth?
                • In what ways do objects tell stories about identity, place, and memory?
                • How do collaboration and community-based practices build relationships and shape the meanings of an artwork?
                • What connections can we make between learning and creative assembling?

                Collect, Assemble, Gather brings together artworks from the Artists for Kids and the Gordon Smith Gallery Permanent Collection, alongside contributions from contemporary Canadian artists and student artists from across the Lower Mainland. The exhibition features works by Kenojuak Ashevak, Sara-Jeanne Bourget, Victor Cicansky, Douglas Coupland, Christos Dikeakos, Stan Douglas, Sylvan Hamburger, Sesemiya, Roz Marshall, Michelle Sound, Brendan Tang, Marlene Yuen, Gu Xiong, Robert Young, the T&T Collective (Tyler Brett and Tony Romano), the Vancouver School Collective, Samuel Roy-Bois, Jack Shadbolt, and Gordon Smith. Perspectives from elementary and secondary students from the North Vancouver and Vancouver School Districts are also featured through linocut prints and collaborative collections presented in the Mezzanine Gallery.

                      History

                      Artists for Kids was founded in 1989 with the singular intent to support art education, for all.

                      In 1990, with the support of art educators and artist patrons Bill Reid, Jack Shadbolt, and Gordon Smith, the first Artists for Kids Limited Edition Portfolio print, Xhuwaji / Haida Grizzly was published - marking the beginning of an extraordinary partnership between Canadian artists and Artists for Kids.

                      Entering its 37th year, Artists for Kids continues to work with renowned Canadian artists and print studios across Canada to publish contemporary limited editions; its portfolio is now revered as one of the most significant limited edition collections in Canada.

                                  Established in 1989 by Canadian artists and educators, Artists for Kids fosters innovative programming at the Gordon Smith Gallery and within the community.                                   The Gordon and Marion Smith Foundation for Young Artists was founded in 2002 to establish an endowment fund, the interest from which would fund ongoing visual arts enrichment opportunities through Artists for Kids.
